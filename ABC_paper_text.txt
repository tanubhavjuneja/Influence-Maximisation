 A B S T R A C T
As one of the fundamental topics within complex networks, the influence maximization problem invites 
the pursuit of key nodes that can maximize the influence spread. However, conventional meta-heuristics 
struggle to identify an optimal seed set due to rigid neighborhood generation strategies and weak directional 
guidance. In light of this, the artificial bee colony algorithm empowered by the variable neighborhood search 
strategy is deployed to address the above challenges. Specifically, an elite-global cooperative neighborhood 
construction mechanism that injects directional cues is devised to avoid aimless exploration. Then, a variable 
neighborhood search strategy that dynamically switches among a continuum of fine-grained neighborhood 
structures is introduced to achieve a good trade-off between exploration and exploitation. Furthermore, when 
the population converges prematurely to local optima, a fine-grained, individual-centric stagnation control 
mechanism combined with a stagnation-aware population management mechanism further maintains search 
vitality at both the individual and population levels. Experiments on nine real-world networks reveal that 
the proposed algorithm achieves an overall average improvement of 6.3% in influence spread relative to 
all baseline methods, demonstrating its strong scalability across different types of networks. Such findings 
showcase its potential for practical applications in rumor control and cascading failures.
1. Introduction
How can a single post shift public perception, yet large-scale cam-
paigns fall silent? Such a pronounced asymmetry compels us to ques-
tion how to accurately locate the source of information dissemination. 
It can be answered via complex network modeling that maps sophis-
ticated social structures onto graphs. In practice, however, the effort 
to detect these source nodes within the modeled graph resembles 
traversing a maze. The challenge lies in going through the maze to find 
these key nodes that can trigger such widespread impact. The objective 
demands both advanced strategies and profound insights into network 
structure [1]. Such complexity not only poses computational challenges 
but also grants the problem remarkable value across multiple practical 
sectors. The ability to pinpoint influential nodes shapes public opinion 
into reasoned dialogue, halts the ripple of failures in power grids, tips 
the scales of elections, and stirs the heart of social movements [2]. 
In quieter corners, this subtle art empowers institutions like hospitals 
to weave vital health notices that shield communities and nurture 
well-being via social media [3].

To formalize the search mathematically, scholars in the domain 
of complex networks framed the issue as the influence maximization 
(IM) problem. The goal is to select initial users within the network, 
often termed seeds, that maximize the influence spread under specific 
propagation models [4]. However, it is far from trivial to identify the 
optimal seed set. Such difficulty arises because every node exhibits 
a distinct influence spreading capability that determines its potential 
to affect neighbors. The hurdle lies not just in finding out influential 
nodes, but in determining how their individual potentials combine to 
maximize the overall spread.
The IM problem was first defined as a discrete combinatorial op-
timization problem with its NP-hardness proved by Kempe et al. [5]. 
It indicates that finding an optimal solution within polynomial time is 
intractable. Early efforts focused on greedy algorithms with provable 
approximation guarantees, but such algorithms are burdened by the 
weight of extensive Monte Carlo simulations to select influential nodes 
one by one. In pursuit of greater computational efficiency, heuristic 
methods [6] that draw upon network topology features have been 
developed to avoid costly simulations. However, the reliance on di-
rect top-ranked selection inherently overlooks influence overlap within

seed sets, thereby undermining performance. To bridge this gap, deep 
learning based methods [7] explicitly integrate the graph topology to 
mitigate such influence overlap. Nevertheless, the gain in precision 
comes with the drawback of prohibitively high training costs.
Distinct from these approaches, meta-heuristics that operate inde-
pendently of problem characteristics have long been valued for tackling 
NP-hard problems [8]. Such algorithms mimic the subtle ingenuity of 
nature to traverse solution spaces with a dynamic equilibrium between 
exploration and exploitation. Many meta-heuristics have been success-
fully applied to IM with promising results, including natural evolution 
strategies such as the memetic algorithm [9], swarm intelligence be-
haviors like harris’ hawks optimization [10], and physical processes 
exemplified by the gravitational search algorithm [11]. Despite their 
prevalence, such algorithms tend to yield seed sets with low accuracy 
due to three primary flaws. Firstly, blind exploitation arises from the 
absence of directional guidance. Secondly, single neighborhood search 
strategies erode population diversity. Thirdly, curtailed exploration in 
later iterations amplifies the risk of premature convergence.
To address the aforementioned challenges, a collaborative optimiza-
tion is pioneered, termed the artificial bee colony algorithm empow-
ered by the variable neighborhood search strategy (ABCVNS). The 
artificial bee colony algorithm (ABC) [12] is adopted as the base 
optimizer due to the alignment between its perturbation logic and 
the node replacement operation in IM. However, ABC’s inherent ran-
domness often traps the search in directional blindness. To rectify 
such low-efficiency disturbances, an elite-global cooperative neighbor-
hood construction (EGCNC) mechanism serves as a directional en-
gine that applies elite knowledge to fine-tune search direction re-
cursively. Although the EGCNC mechanism enhances exploitation, ef-
fective optimization requires a good trade-off between exploitation 
and exploration. Thus, the variable neighborhood search (VNS) strat-
egy [13] is introduced to achieve such a trade-off. The VNS strategy 
enables dynamic switching among neighborhood structures, unlike ex-
isting meta-heuristics that are confined to a single and fixed neighbor-
hood search pattern. Such flexibility in the neighborhood structures 
augments ABCVNS’s capacity to escape local optima.
Moreover, a dual-level scheme is adopted to mitigate the risk 
of premature convergence. At the individual level, a fine-grained, 
individual-centric stagnation control mechanism is devised by ABC’s 
unique abandonment strategy that resets a specific failing individual 
without disrupting the population in real-time. At the population level, 
a stagnation-aware population management mechanism responds to 
the diversity decay. Finally, a robust, interpretable, and generalizable 
solving paradigm is established to solve the IM and open a universal 
pathway to tackle other combinatorial optimization problems. The 
major contributions of our method are described as follows:
• The ABC empowered by VNS is introduced as a novel optimiza-
tion to navigate the rugged IM landscape. Specifically, diverse 
neighborhood structures are dynamically switched to overcome 
the local optima traps and underpin a robust balance between 
exploitation and exploration.
• The directional blindness of standard ABC is resolved by an elite-
global cooperative neighborhood construction (EGCNC) mecha-
nism to transform the search from aimless wandering into elite-
oriented fine-tuning.
• A fine-grained, individual-centric stagnation control mechanism 
is established by uniquely employing ABC’s abandonment strat-
egy to bridge the gap in resolving the individual stagnation in 
real-time.

2. Related work
2.1. Approximate methods
Kempe et al. [5] set forth a hill-climbing greedy algorithm for the 
IM, crowned with a (1 − 1∕𝑒 − 𝜖) approximation guarantee, where 𝜖 ac-
counts for the error introduced by Monte Carlo simulations. However, 
the need for such extensive simulations per node to compute marginal 
gains incurred 𝑂(𝑘𝑅𝑚𝑛) complexity, where 𝑘, 𝑅, 𝑛, and 𝑚 denote the 
seed set size, the simulation count, the number of nodes, and edges in 
the graph, respectively. Leskovec et al. [14] answered the time hurdle 
with the Cost-Effective Lazy-Forward algorithm (CELF). The authors 
exploited the submodularity of the objective function to prune redun-
dant computations and accomplished a 700-fold speedup. But repeated 
marginal gain computations persisted as a scalability defect. Chen et al. 
[15] extended the method by a node exclusion rule to further lower 
complexity to 𝑂(𝑘𝑅𝑚) under the well-established independent cascade 
(IC) model. Even with such enhancements, simulation-based methods 
remain computationally expensive for large-scale networks.
A paradigm shift occurred with Borgs et al. [16], who resolved the 
barrier with reverse influence sampling (RIS) and marked the first near-
linear-time algorithm with theoretical guarantees. Since then, subse-
quent research has focused on optimizing RIS efficiency. Notably, Tang 
et al. [17] implemented a two-phase influence maximization method 
to reduce time complexity, later devising the improved version IMM 
based on martingale estimation technology [18]. And Guo et al. [19] 
optimized reverse reachable set generation via subset sampling and 
sentinel strategies to drastically reduce both the sampling overhead and 
the average size of reverse reachable sets. Despite their accuracy, RIS-
based methods exhibit scalability constraints dominated by memory 
space. This is because all generated reverse reachable sets must be 
retained in memory for the subsequent seed selection phase.
At the core, the first category of paradigms remains undermined 
by dual implementation barriers: prohibitive computational expenses 
from repeated simulations and sustainable memory demands. These 
shortcomings highlight the pressing need for algorithms that balance 
accuracy and scalability.
2.2. Heuristic methods
Heuristic methods respond to the efficiency bottlenecks by screening 
influential nodes based on topological metrics ranging from local to 
semi-local and global. Local metrics, e.g., degree centrality [20], are 
simple and fast but fail to capture influence beyond immediate neigh-
bors. Conversely, global indicators like closeness centrality [21] assess 
node influence more precisely, albeit with prohibitive computational 
complexity. To this end, Chen and Su [22] engineered a novel metric 
named DC+ and the corresponding gravity model extensions DCGM+, 
which quantified influence via the node and neighbor degrees to resolve 
ambiguities in uniform-degree nodes. Recently, Zhu and Hao [23] 
fused intrinsic centrality combining k-shell with degree information, 
with extrinsic centrality capturing neighbor contributions. It achieved 
linear complexity 𝑂(𝑚), though its performance remained partially con-
strained by the sensitivity of k-shell decomposition. While automatic 
heuristic design has been applied to many NP-hard problems [24], re-
search in IM has primarily focused on manually engineering composite 
metrics.
Heuristic methods offer a tractable path toward the IM in large-scale 
networks. However, a critical limitation is that heuristics directly select 
the top-𝑘 highest-scoring nodes as the seed set without adequately con-
sidering potential influence overlap among candidates. The oversight 
constrains propagation effectiveness and makes performance sensitive 
to network topology.

2.3. Meta-heuristic methods
Characterized by low dependency on prior knowledge and sweeping 
global search capabilities, meta-heuristics [25] have emerged to over-
come structural sensitivity and influence overlap in heuristics. Based on 
their underlying principles, meta-heuristics can be divided into three 
classes: physics-based algorithms, evolutionary algorithms, and swarm 
intelligence algorithms.
Physics-based algorithms constitute the first primary class, with 
simulated annealing (SA) being the most representative example. Jiang 
et al. [26] pioneered its application to IM and proposed the expected 
diffusion value (EDV) function to bypass costly simulations. Although 
SA theoretically balances exploration and exploitation via temperature 
control, its fixed cooling rate and sensitivity to the initial temperature 
compromise robustness. Later variants incorporating adaptive tempera-
ture tuning [27] were designed to optimize both convergence rates and 
stability.
Inspired by Darwin’s theory of evolution, evolutionary algorithms 
emulate genetic operators to guide the search process. Qiu et al. [28] 
devised LIDDE, a local influence decreasing differential evolution al-
gorithm that considers fourth-order neighbor evaluation to sharpen 
precision. But the fixed candidate pool sizes constrain adaptability. Zhu 
et al. [29] addressed the flaws with a phased evaluation-enhanced 
approach, PHEE, by constructing a dynamic pool sizing based on 
network scale. Parameter sensitivity, however, remains a drawback. 
More recently, Tang et al. [30] augmented the differential evolution 
algorithm with fitness landscape analysis and directed the search via 
entropy, albeit with added landscape sampling costs.
Distinct from evolutionary algorithms, swarm intelligence algo-
rithms exploit swarm behaviors rather than genetic mutation to nav-
igate the solution space. Inspired by the foraging behaviors of birds,
Gong et al. [31] presented a discrete particle swarm optimization 
algorithm and constructed a two-hop influence evaluation function to 
estimate the influence spread of candidate seeds, though computation-
ally expensive. To expedite convergence, He et al. [32] proposed a 
multisearch particle swarm optimization algorithm that incorporates 
learning automata to coordinate multiple search strategies. Beyond 
particle swarm optimization, ABC has emerged as another prominent 
swarm intelligence algorithm. Improvements to ABC primarily target 
evaluation function design and search strategy optimization. As for 
assessment accuracy, Ma and Liu [33] developed an adaptive discrete 
ABC algorithm (ADABC) that incorporates a three-layer evaluation 
strategy, but its computational overhead hinders scalability. Another 
improvement by Sheikhahmadi and Zareie [34] architected a multi-
objective ABC (MOABC) that embedded a dynamic cross-variation 
mechanism to bolster search capabilities.
In summary, despite the progress achieved, existing approaches 
still face the following shortcomings. First, blind exploitation arises 
from the absence of directional guidance. Second, single neighborhood 
search strategies erode population diversity. Third, curtailed explo-
ration in later iterations amplifies the risk of premature convergence. 
All these shortcomings limit the upper bound of the algorithm’s solution 
accuracy.

While the aforementioned vector-based perturbation works well 
for continuous optimization problems, it is not directly applicable 
to combinatorial optimization problems like IM, where solutions are 
represented as sets of nodes. More importantly, the neighborhood 
generation strategy in standard ABC (Eq. (4)) remains driven by the 
stochastic difference between two random individuals. The absence of 
elite guidance in such a reliance on arbitrary differences impedes the 
discovery of promising areas and is prone to premature convergence. 
Existing improvements, such as the comprehensive-learning guided up-
dating rule in ADABC [33], attempt to bridge this gap but are hindered 
by their sensitivity to the population sampling ratio. In particular, the 
use of a fixed sampling proportion across different networks limits the 
algorithm’s performance ceiling.
To overcome these drawbacks, the EGCNC mechanism is devised 
to generate candidates. Unlike traditional methods that rely on blind 
perturbations, the EGCNC mechanism explicitly integrates the current 
solution with informative structures from better-performing individu-
als. The integration is realized through a three-stage process. First, an 
elite set 𝐸𝑆 is dynamically constructed to identify superior individuals. 
Second, a candidate pool 𝐶𝑆 is adaptively established through a set-
based union of the current solution and elite structures. Finally, a 
new candidate is derived via uniform sampling from 𝐶𝑆 , ensuring 
a functional fusion of existing knowledge and elite guidance. The 
implementation details for each stage are as follows.
Firstly, let 𝐸𝑆 denote the set of elite solutions in the population 𝑃
whose influence, measured by the LIE function (Eq. (2)), exceeds that 
of the current solution 𝑆𝑖, as formalized in Eq. (5):

The effectiveness of the EGCNC mechanism is rooted in three key 
principles. First, search efficiency is enhanced through search space 
pruning. The EGCNC mechanism prunes the search space by restrict-
ing candidates to a high-potential pool 𝐶𝑆 composed of nodes from 
elite solutions. The search ignores less promising nodes, thereby ac-
celerating convergence toward high-quality regions. Second, algorithm 
performance is improved through the recombination of advantageous 
genes. Since the candidate pool 𝐶𝑆 aggregates nodes proven effec-
tive in superior solutions, the sampling process from 𝐶𝑆 acts as a 
recombination of advantageous genes. Instead of trying a random new 
node, the algorithm combines successful genes from multiple superior 
peers. Such collective guidance increases the probability of discovering 
superior solutions, leading to a higher final influence spread. Third, 
search capability is strengthened via adaptive guidance. The adaptive 
switching between 𝐸𝑆 and 𝐺best ensures consistent search pressure to 
keep the search directed toward the promising region discovered so 
far. Simultaneously, the stochastic nature of the sampling from 𝐶𝑆
introduces a necessary variation to escape local optima and maintain 
population diversity. The efficacy of the EGCNC mechanism is em-
pirically validated in Section 4.3.The integration of EGCNC into the

3.4. Variable neighborhood search
The fixed, single neighborhood search pattern used in the employed 
and onlooker bee phases of the fundamental ABC restricts effectiveness 
in sophisticated landscapes. Prompted by the insight, a VNS-oriented 
neighborhood search strategy is embedded into the ABC to help the 
algorithm search the space efficiently and improve solution quality.
3.4.1. Principles
The core principle of the VNS strategy [13] is to systematically 
change neighborhood structures during the search process. It is
grounded on three key facts [35]. First, a local optimum with respect 
to one neighborhood structure may not be a local optimum for another. 
Second, a global optimum is a local optimum with respect to all possible 
neighborhood structures. Third, for many problems, local optima with 
respect to one or several neighborhoods are relatively close to each 
other. The VNS strategy leverages these facts by alternating among 
neighborhood structures of varying scales, thereby possessing signifi-
cant potential to escape local optima and explore broader regions of the 
solution space. Such an ability is particularly vital for the IM problem, 
which is characterized by multi-modality and rugged landscapes. In 
such a complex environment, a single-scale neighborhood search is 
highly susceptible to premature convergence. By contrast, the multi-
neighborhood search of VNS provides a robust mechanism to facilitate 
traversal of the rugged terrain, reducing the likelihood of being trapped 
in stagnation.
Generally, VNS variants are distinguished by their neighborhood 
switching strategies and solution acceptance rules. Common neigh-
borhood switching strategies include sequential change step and cyclic 
change step [36]. In the progressive stage, the algorithm adopts the
sequential change step to facilitate a stable, progressive search. However, 
in the plateau stage, the switching shifts to a cyclic change step to explore 
diverse neighborhoods, entailing extra time, as detailed in Section 3.5. 
To mitigate such an overhead and ensure efficiency, the study uses the
first improvement strategy rather than the computationally intensive best 
improvement strategy [37].
3.4.2. Neighborhood structure design
A three-layer neighborhood hierarchy (1, 2, 3) is adopted to 
balance exploration capacity with computational overhead. On one 
hand, a structure with too few layers narrows the search spectrum 
and weakens the capacity to break free from local traps, potentially 
compromising solution quality. On the other hand, an excessive number 
of layers incurs redundant switching overhead due to frequent struc-
ture switching, diminishing the algorithm’s efficiency. Therefore, the 
proposed three-layer hierarchy embodies a deliberate trade-off between 
perturbation granularity and computational tractability.
Each layer is theoretically aligned to a specific structural scale 
of complex networks. Such a hierarchical alignment between search 
operators and network topology enables the search to transcend single-
scale limitations. While all three layers employ a node-replacement 
mechanism, they differ fundamentally in the topological scope of the 
candidate pool and the evaluation metrics. Specifically, 1 focuses 
on a fine-grained refinement within the first-hop neighborhood. 2
broadens the horizon to the two-hop neighborhood, targeting bridges 
invisible to 1. Unlike 1 and 2, which are constrained by proximity 
to 𝑆, 3 enacts jumps across communities to push the search into 
distant regions. The transition from 𝑁1(𝑆) to 𝑁2(𝑆) and further to 
cross-community candidate sets introduces progressively larger trans-
formation radii to systematically expand the reachable solution regions. 
Such a multi-scale transition mechanism allows the search to broaden 
the exploration horizon, encompassing solutions that are inaccessi-
ble to single-neighborhood searches, thereby reducing the likelihood 
of premature convergence. The detailed operational mechanisms and 
search operators for each layer are elaborated below, beginning with 
the fine-grained refinement of 1.

The execution logic of the VNS strategy centers on the iterative re-

Local search. To ensure 𝑆′ reaches its full potential, a local search

First, nodes in 𝑆′ are ranked in descending order by 𝑆𝑐𝑜𝑟𝑒 defined in

Solution acceptance. The transition from 𝑆 to 𝑆′′ is governed by a 
strict improvement rule. Only when 𝐿𝐼𝐸 (𝑆′′) > 𝐿𝐼𝐸 (𝑆′) is the incum-

against quality degradation evoked by randomness and ensures stable 
convergence.
Adaptive neighborhood change step and termination. The
neighborhood change step adapts to the evolutionary state of the ABC 
to balance search depth and computational cost.
• Sequential mode (Set boolean flag 𝑐𝑦𝑐𝑙𝑖𝑐 = False): Prioritizes ef-
ficiency. This mode is preferred when the global best solution 
is steadily evolving to save costs. If improved, the process exits

concludes immediately upon an improvement being successfully 
captured or the neighborhood index 𝑚 exceeds the maximum tier

ness. Activated during the stagnation stage, this mode ensures 
an exhaustive traversal of all defined neighborhood structures 
(1 − 3) to maximize the probability of escaping local optima. 
Even if an improvement is found early, the process proceeds to 
the next neighborhood tier (𝑚 ← 𝑚 + 1). 𝑆 is updated whenever 
an improvement is found, but termination occurs only when the

In summary, the proposed VNS strategy acts as an independent 
plugin that is embedded into the ABC framework, designed to achieve 
a good trade-off between exploration and exploitation. Based on the 
theoretical principle that a local optimum in one neighborhood is not 
necessarily a local optimum in another, the mechanism designs a three-
tier progressive neighborhood structure (1, 2, 3), which expands 
the exploration range of the solution space by increasing the search 
radius. These larger perturbation radii facilitate the escape from the 
current attraction basin into unexplored regions. Subsequently, a local 
search operator is employed for deep exploitation to accelerate the 
convergence. Furthermore, the adaptive neighborhood switching steps 
adjust the mode based on the evolutionary state of the ABC to balance 
search depth and computational overhead. During periods of contin-
uous improvement in the global best solution, the sequential mode is 
adopted following the first improvement principle to conserve compu-
tational overhead. In contrast, during stagnation, the mode switches to 
cyclic to traverse all neighborhoods, which enhances the probability 
of escaping local traps. Such a theoretical framework coordinates the 
dynamic balance between global exploration and local exploitation, 
thereby achieving a balance between computational cost and solution 
quality. The effectiveness of the design is validated in Section 4.3.
3.5. Population management

solutions, it does not inherently prevent the decline of population di-

iterations, manifested by the global best solution remaining unchanged 
for prolonged periods and a sharp decline in population diversity. Such 
a phenomenon arises as exploration weakens and the search space nar-
rows, forcing solutions to converge toward similar structures. Although 
ABC hinges on scout bees to promote exploration, the mechanism fails 
to resolve the issue since it is triggered only after a fixed number of 
unsuccessful trials.

4. Experimental results and analysis
This section systematically evaluates ABCVNS that covers experi-
mental settings, module-wise ablation studies, and comparative anal-
yses against up-to-date algorithms. All procedures were encoded in 
Python and executed on a Windows 11 server equipped with an Intel(R) 
Core(TM) i9-14900HX processor (2.2 GHz) and 32 GB of RAM.
4.1. Experimental settings
Datasets. Nine real-world network datasets with diverse domains 
and structures are utilized to validate the algorithm’s effectiveness and 
robustness. The topological properties are summarized in Table  1. All 
datasets are publicly available and can be freely downloaded from 
https://www.example.com and https://networkrepository.com/index.
php.
Baselines. A rigorous demonstration of the superiority and robust-
ness of the proposed algorithm is achieved by comparisons with a 
comprehensive suite of baselines ranging from classical greedy ap-
proximations to recent state-of-the-art meta-heuristics. The selection 
rationales are as follows: (1) CELF [14], renowned for a classic greedy 
algorithm with a theoretical guarantee, serves as a benchmark for 
accuracy to evaluate the solution quality of the proposed algorithm. 
(2) DCGM+ [22], a representative high-efficiency heuristic, is selected 
to demonstrate that the proposed algorithm achieves superior solution 
quality without sacrificing computational speed. (3) LIDDE [28], an 
evolutionary algorithm, is included to assess performance against com-
petitive meta-heuristics. (4) PHEE [29], a recent hybrid competitor that 
integrates simulated annealing to refine search outcomes, is chosen to 
test the proposed algorithm’s effectiveness. (5) ADABC [33], the most 
advanced ABC variant, is used to verify the specific improvements of 
our proposed mechanisms over existing ABC frameworks. (6) MFEA-
RCIM [38], a pioneering multifactorial evolutionary framework for the 
IM variants, is included to evaluate whether the proposed algorithm can 
achieve competitive performance compared to advanced optimization 
paradigms that utilize inter-task knowledge transfer and latent task 
synergies. (7) MFEA-RCIMMD [39], a state-of-the-art extension featuring 
multi-phase transfer strategies, is selected to benchmark the proposed 
algorithm’s performance and its search efficiency in handling large-
scale and complex search spaces. For a fair comparison under a unified 
problem setting, methods originally designed for more complex vari-
ants of IM are adapted to the IM by aligning their objective functions, 
while preserving their core optimization mechanisms.
Parameters configuration. The ABCVNS algorithm operates under 
seven principal parameters, namely maximum iterations 𝑔max, pop-
ulation size 𝑁𝑃 , abandonment limit for food sources 𝐿, stagnation 
threshold 𝜏max, tournament size 𝑡𝑠, retention ratio 𝑟, and local search 
evaluation count 𝑐𝑛. Optimal parameter values in the study were iden-
tified via an orthogonal experimental design in Section 4.2. Further-
more, for fairness, all comparative algorithms strictly adhere to the 
parameter settings and technical implementation details prescribed 
by their original authors. Each method is exhaustively executed 30 
times for reliability, and the reported results correspond to the average 
performance across runs.

4.4. Comparative analysis
4.4.1. Performance on influence spread
Influence diffusion was traced across varying seed set sizes to shed 
light on propagation dynamics. To ground the observation in quanti-
tative terms, the influence spread for 𝑘 ∈ [10 , 100] was estimated via 
1000 Monte Carlo simulations, with values averaged over 30 indepen-
dent runs per algorithm. Experimental configurations included an edge 
activation probability of 𝑝 = 0 .05 under the IC model for the seven 
smaller networks (Blog, CA-GrQc, Lastfm, CA-HepTh, Twin, NetHEHT, 
Deezer), while a probability of 𝑝 = 0 .01 was adopted for the two rela-
tively large-scale networks, Soc-slashdot and Soc-delicious, to mitigate 
computational overhead. Community structures were extracted through 
the 𝐿𝑜𝑢𝑣𝑎𝑖𝑛 algorithm [40]. Section 4.1 outlines the selection rationale 
and characteristics of the compared algorithms. Finally, Fig.  5 presents 
the results of eight algorithms on nine networks, where the 𝑥 and 𝑦 axes 
denote seed size and influence spread, respectively.
Analysis of the results in Fig.  5 clearly shows that the proposed 
algorithm slightly underperforms CELF but surpasses the other algo-
rithms. In Figs.  5(a), (d), (e), (g), (h), and (i), ABCVNS’s performance 
rivals that of the greedy algorithm. The high accuracy benefits from 
the well-grained VNS strategy that prevents the algorithm from getting 
stuck in local optima within a single neighborhood. In Fig.  5(b), the 
performance gap is observed in the CA-GrQc network at 𝑘 = 100, with 
a difference of 9.1% due to the network’s relatively high assortativ-
ity. Nodes in such a high assortativity network tend to connect with 
others of similar degree, limiting the diversity of reachable neighbors 
and making exploration more difficult. In contrast, DCGM+ performs 
the weakest, ranking last in all networks with a noticeably smaller 
influence spread, and its gains diminish as the seed set size increases. 
ABCVNS improves influence spread by approximately 40.67% (Blog), 
64.49% (CA-GrQc), 27.91% (Lastfm), 26.56% (CA-HepTh), 25.63% 
(Twin), 18.18% (NetHEHT), 16.66% (Deezer), 8.11% (Soc-slashdot), 
and 18.71% (Soc-delicious) on average compared to DCGM+. The 
shortcoming arises primarily because heuristics directly select the top-
ranked as seeds, overlooking the influence overlap between seeds. A 
closer inspection reveals notable differences in how each algorithm 
scales with seed size. Across all networks, the influence spread of eight 
algorithms generally increases with larger seed sets, confirming that 
additional seed nodes facilitate broader diffusion. However, regarding 
DCGM+, as 𝑘 grows, the influence overlap between newly added seeds 
and existing nodes can undermine the overall performance.
In Figs.  5(a), (c), (f), (g), and (i), LIDDE and ADABC exhibit compa-
rable performance, while PHEE achieves superior results. The former’s 
reliance on a fixed-size candidate pool insufficiently adapts to network 
scale and seed set size. In comparison, owing to the fact that its 
dynamical adjustments of the candidate pool, PHEE leads to better 
performance relative to the fixed-pool counterpart. But the reliance 
on k-core values hinders accurate assessment of node influence. As a 
result, the construction of the candidate node pool can be compro-
mised. ABCVNS addresses the above limitation by leveraging network 
structural information to construct the candidate pool. Such a design 
improves the robustness of the algorithm by reducing the impact of in-
accurate candidate node selection. Relative to existing meta-heuristics, 
ABCVNS demonstrates an increase of 3.39% on Blog, 8.39% on CA-
GrQc, 4% on Lastfm, 4.61% on CA-HepTh, 4.84% on Twin, 4.42% 
on NetHEHT, 2.67% on Deezer, 2.61% on Soc-slashdot, and 1.95% 
Soc-delicious. These experimental results across these diverse network 
structural characteristics demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness 
of our algorithm.
The correlation between network topology and algorithmic per-
formance can be observed across the tested datasets. In networks 
exhibiting high degree heterogeneity, such as Soc-delicious, where the 
maximum degree exceeds 3200, the presence of hub nodes tends to bias 
heuristic strategies toward selecting high-degree nodes. It often results 
in a concentration of seeds within densely connected regions, leading 
to substantial influence overlap and consequently limiting marginal 
gain, which increases the risk of convergence to local optima. The 
incorporation of VNS within ABCVNS alleviates such a bias by intro-
ducing structural diversity into the search process, thereby reducing 
the dependence on hub-dominated regions.
In addition, structural properties such as assortativity and clustering 
coefficients further affect the search landscape. Networks with high 
clustering coefficient and positive assortativity, such as CA-GrQc, typ-
ically exhibit strong community structure, where high-degree nodes 
are more likely to connect with each other. Such a structural pattern 
restricts diffusion paths to intra-community regions and constrains 
the propagation toward peripheral nodes. The use of neighborhood 
structures within the VNS strategy supports transitions across differ-
ent structural regions, allowing candidate solutions to explore nodes 
beyond densely connected clusters. Conversely, in networks with rela-
tively homogeneous degree distributions and low average degree, such 
as Twin, the absence of prominent hubs results in a more uniform distri-
bution of influence potential across nodes. Under such conditions, the 
marginal contribution of individual nodes is limited, and the influence 
spread depends more on the joint effect of node combinations. Conse-
quently, the search process requires a balanced coordination between 
exploration and exploitation, where multiple candidate combinations 
need to be evaluated to capture the interaction effects among nodes that 
govern the diffusion process. These observations are consistent with the 
experimental results reported in Fig.  5, where ABCVNS demonstrates 
more stable performance in networks with high degree heterogeneity 
and clustering.

5. Discussion
Comparison with existing work. First, regarding efficiency and 
accuracy trade-offs, as noted in the Introduction section, traditional 
greedy algorithms are often constrained by the prohibitive computa-
tional overhead of extensive Monte Carlo simulations, despite offering 
provable approximation guarantees. Conversely, heuristics tend to pro-
duce inferior solutions due to their oversight of influence overlap 
among the candidate seeds. ABCVNS leverages the fitness function 
to avoid costly simulations, achieving a more favorable balance be-
tween efficiency and accuracy. Second, in terms of search strategy, 
considering that existing meta-heuristics rely on a single and fixed 
neighborhood search pattern, the proposed algorithm applies the VNS 
strategy with multiple alternating neighborhood operators effectively 
to explore the search space. Third, concerning stagnation resolution, 
the proposed algorithm distinguishes itself by leveraging a fine-grained, 
individual-centric stagnation control strategy to specifically target and 
resolve stagnation at the individual level. Complementary to this, a 
stagnation-aware population management mechanism regenerates less 
promising individuals from the population-level to sustain search vi-
tality over extended iterations. Finally, empirical validation on nine 
real-world networks demonstrates that ABCVNS exhibits strong perfor-
mance comparable to greedy baselines but with orders of magnitude 
faster convergence across varying topologies.
Theoretical contributions. Methodologically, the proposed algo-
rithm unveils a population-driven intelligent optimization that offers a 
configurable and extensible template for other combinatorial optimiza-
tion problems. First, we propose the EGCNC mechanism to overcome 
the absence of directional guidance in standard ABC perturbation logic. 
By embedding information from elite and globally optimal individuals 
into neighborhood perturbations, the EGCNC mechanism concentrates 
exploration on promising regions. Furthermore, the bifurcated scout 
strategy retains high-quality nodes from global best solutions to prevent 
random generation from disrupting the structure of the optimal solution 
in the current population. Finally, the study theoretically validates the 
efficacy of tournament selection within the IM, demonstrating that 
maintaining moderate selection pressure is crucial for preventing the 
algorithm from becoming trapped in deceptive local optima.
Practical implications. ABCVNS is applicable to decision-making 
problems in real-world networked systems, where the selection of a 
limited set of critical nodes is required under resource constraints. 
In rumor suppression, the problem can be formulated as choosing a 
subset of users whose control minimizes the propagation of misinfor-
mation, and ABCVNS addresses this task by identifying high-impact 
nodes that serve as prioritized intervention targets, providing a princi-
pled basis for allocating resources toward fact-checking dissemination, 
content regulation, or exposure control. The algorithm is designed to 
support decision-making rather than operate as a fully autonomous 
system. It offers a ranked candidate set that can be evaluated based 
on contextual or domain-specific criteria before intervention, thereby 
establishing a human-in-the-loop paradigm that facilitates more reli-
able and context-aware interventions. Similarly, in infrastructure net-
works, operators face the challenge of mitigating cascading failures 
under limited monitoring and maintenance capacity. ABCVNS identifies 
critical components whose failure may induce widespread disruption, 
providing guidance for prioritizing inspection, reinforcement, or load 
redistribution to reduce systemic risk while maintaining operational 
efficiency. In political campaigning, the task involves selecting a subset 
of individuals to maximize information dissemination under budget and 
resource constraints. ABCVNS provides a ranked set of influential nodes 
that enables targeted allocation of outreach efforts toward individuals 
with higher propagation potential.
Extension to Dynamic Networks. The proposed ABCVNS models 
the dynamic network as a sequence of network snapshots, which is a 
common representation where the network evolves over discrete time 
steps [41]. Such a formulation aligns with practical constraints where 
computational resources are limited and strategic decisions, such as 
phased product promotion campaigns or scheduled infrastructure main-
tenance, are naturally implemented in discrete stages. The effectiveness 
of the proposed approach relies on the observation that the set of 
influential nodes tends to remain relatively stable across consecutive 
snapshots, as observed in [42]. ABCVNS leverages the above property 
by using high-quality solutions from previous time steps to initialize 
the search for the current snapshot, rather than restarting from scratch. 
Consequently, the search is guided toward promising regions of the 
solution space with reduced computational overhead. The VNS strategy 
further facilitates localized refinements, allowing the population to 
adapt to structural changes and progressively converge toward up-
dated optima. By leveraging solution reuse across sequential snapshots, 
ABCVNS strikes a robust balance between computational tractability 
and solution adaptability in continuously evolving networks.
Limitations and future work. Although ABCVNS achieves better 
solution performance compared with other methods, it is important to 
acknowledge that the introduction of the VNS strategy inevitably incurs

additional computational costs due to iterative local search proce-
dures. Future work could investigate adaptive tuning of neighborhood 
structures to retain solution quality with reduced runtime, especially 
for large-scale networks. Moreover, the proposed algorithm currently 
focuses on single-objective optimization. However, real-world scenar-
ios often involve competing objectives, such as maximizing influence 
while simultaneously minimizing advertising costs. Future efforts could 
formulate multi-objective variants of ABCVNS.
6. Conclusion
This paper revitalizes ABC by blending it with the VNS strategy 
and culminates in a novel intelligent optimization algorithm termed 
ABCVNS. The algorithm applies the VNS strategy to achieve a dynamic 
neighborhood switching scheme to strengthen the capacity in local 
search. To further steer the search toward promising regions, a well-
designed elite-global cooperative neighborhood construction mecha-
nism is devised to adjust search directions based on historical infor-
mation. Extensive experiments on nine networks demonstrate that our 
algorithm surpasses the state-of-the-art algorithms on various types 
of networks. These outstanding findings highlight the potential of 
ABCVNS for IM. Although validated on the IM, its methodological 
advancements generalize to a variety of combinatorial optimization 
tasks characterized by curtailed neighborhood diversity or weakened 
late-stage search performance. The merits underscore the theoretical 
contribution of advancing the baseline algorithm and offer method-
ological support for social network analysis and swarm intelligence 
optimization. Despite these strengths, the algorithm currently faces 
limitations. The present study is subjected to single-layer networks with 
a single-objective focus. Future efforts could extend the approach to 
higher-order networks and formulate multi-objective variants to better 
capture complex real-world scenarios.
